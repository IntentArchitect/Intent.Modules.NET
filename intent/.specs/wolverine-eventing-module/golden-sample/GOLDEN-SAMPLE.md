Two lines, one per application. Both must be removed together with their directive and marker when the owning template starts generating them, and step 3 of the marker sweep applies: regenerate and confirm the template emits the exact line. | 2 | The same call                                                                    | `WolverineEventing.Subscribe.RabbitMQ.Api/Program.cs:45` | `//IntentIgnore` on line 44 | **ignore** | `// GOLDEN-SAMPLE:` trailing on line 45 | As above                                                                                                                 |

**Both rows are ignore-style, and that is the higher-risk form.** Merge-style was preferred and is not available here: `Intent.AspNetCore.Program` emits this file with `[assembly: DefaultIntentManaged(Mode.Fully)]` and, under top-level statements, the registration sits in a statement block with no enclosing member to carry `[IntentManaged(Mode.Merge)]`. The only line-level directive available is `//IntentIgnore`, following the precedent at `Tests/CleanArchitecture.Comprehensive/.../Client/Program.cs:20`.

The consequence to carry forward: because these are ignore-style, a missed cleanup will **silently suppress** the template's output rather than showing up as a visible duplicate. The parity check would pass while proving nothing. That is exactly why the marker sweep is mandatory here rather than advisory.

Marker placement is constrained by how `//IntentIgnore` works — it protects the **next line only**. A marker above the directive sits outside the protected region and is stripped; a marker between the directive and the statement steals the protection and leaves the statement exposed. A trailing comment on the protected line itself is the only provably-inside position, and it is verified by the clean regeneration recorded below.

Sweep command: `grep -rn "GOLDEN-SAMPLE:" Tests/WolverineEventing.Publish.RabbitMQ Tests/WolverineEventing.Subscribe.RabbitMQ` — must return exactly these two lines now, and nothing once the module generates them.

Everything else the sample hand-writes survives regeneration already: new files no template claims, the DI line inside `[IntentMerge]`, the additive appsettings sections, and handler bodies in merge mode.

### Known deviation — do not transcribe this into a template

The **publisher's** `ConfigureHandlerDiscovery` registers `ShipOrderCommandHandler`, `RequestOrderProcessingCommandHandler` and `FailOrderCommandHandler` (`…Publish…/Eventing/WolverineEventingConfiguration.cs:50-52`). All three are CQRS handlers owned by `Intent.Application.Wolverine`. They are registered there only because `DisableConventionalDiscovery()` is called in the same method and would otherwise strand them, and because no common module exists yet to own that call. The intended shape is D5.
